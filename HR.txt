 Faced failure 

While writing a research paper, Our professor assigned us a topic based on a framework he had developed a few years earlier. When we tested it with machine learning, we found that the results were negative, partly because the approach was outdated.

Initially, this was a setback because our expected outcome wasn’t achieved. Instead of trying to manipulate the results or ignore the problem, we decided to understand why it was happening. We then developed a framework to systematically evaluate the impact of such approaches and analyse why machine learning integration could produce negative results.

The biggest lesson I learned was that failure is not always wasted effort. If you analyse it properly, it can help you understand the depth and even help build something more useful.

Academic Projects
I've worked on several academic projects during my B.Tech, mainly in the domains of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and cybersecurity.

Some of these projects have also developed into research papers which are published

One project I'd like to mention is FedCL-NIDS, which focuses on using machine learning (federated learning) to detect suspicious network activity while keeping data distributed across different systems.

1. Dataset: CICIDS2017 network traffic dataset to train and evaluate. Benchmark dataset by reputed Canadian institute 
2. Machine Learning: Used Random Forest (classification) (50 trees, maximum depth 10) and XGBoost learns by building decision trees one after another, Combines the contributions of the trees. Used as a comparison model to evaluate detection capabilities.
3. Federated Learning: Simulated four clients: web server, database, email server, and file server, allowing collaboration without sharing raw network traffic among clients. In order to adhere with GDPR compliances . Flower library used 
4. Security Technologies: Used Snort sniffs packets for finding known attack signatures and YARA for detection-rule generation. Rules required 3 out of 4 votes and a similarity threshold of 0.7 to become global rule.
5. Results: The complete system achieved an average F1-score of 80.52%, improving on the non-federated baseline by 5.88 percentage points.

It was a valuable experience because I got to understand how machine learning concepts can be applied to real-world cybersecurity problems.
